# redkiln-old (Node/TypeScript Redkiln 0.21.0): hard-won lessons

Repo: `/home/user/redkiln-old` at `e61b68f7` (0.21.0 release merge, 2026-08-31), read-only. 2,577 commits; the commits before ~2026-07 are an earlier, unrelated Python/console product, so the Redkiln history proper runs 0.1.0 (2026-06) to 0.21.0. No audit exists for this repo in `references/strategy/audit/`. `references/docs/knowledge.md` already covers the `.kb` schema, `validate --kb` and kb-ingest, and `references/docs/loop.md` notes the templates and prompts as #79 sources. This file covers what those leave out.

The best primary sources in the repo, for whoever writes the #79 checklists or ports Loop:
- `REDKILN-V1-READINESS-REVIEW.md` (2026-06-28, at 0.1.0)
- `references/audit/2026-08-30-state-of-the-system/` (289 weighted findings at 0.20.0; start at `readiness-ruling.md` and `findings/_index.md`)
- `references/2026-07-2*.md` (six dated field bug reports from the Inksmith pilot)
- `references/field-reports/`, `references/MULTI-HARNESS.md`
- `AGENTS.md` (model-tier and effort routing)
- `.gitattributes` (three incident write-ups in its comments)
- `processes/*.yaml` (the comments record reversed decisions)

**Why it was rewritten in Rust** (from `/home/user/redkiln-rs/references/2026-08-31-rust-inversion-RFC.md`, written against this tree at 0.20.0). The language was not the cause. What failed was who held the loop. Control flow lived in two places: prose in `commands/*.md`, which the model had to re-execute correctly on every run (about 50% of the 2,028 command lines were orchestration logic), and sandboxed Workflow-runtime JS (`workflows/forge-*.js`, 13,439 LOC). About two-thirds of that JS was compensation for the sandbox: it had no modules and no filesystem, a single cumulative budget counter, and a resume journal keyed on (prompt, opts). Gates the model was asked to respect got walked past. The rewrite moved the loop into a binary that spawns `claude -p` per step ("Bet 1"). The engine port ("Bet 2") was judged weaker. Its risks were YAML byte-parity, which had no bound, and the permanent loss of "clone and it works" distribution.

## Guardrails

- **[hooks] A PreToolUse hook enforces the single-writer rule for item frontmatter, because prose did not.** The rule that the CLI alone writes system frontmatter was stated in `AGENTS.md` and restated in prompts as `REDKILN_FRONTMATTER_RULE`, and agents broke it anyway. The evidence: `retier` existed only to repair the state that hand-edits produced. 0.12.0 added a `PreToolUse` deny on `Write|Edit|MultiEdit`. A whole-file `Write` is denied outright; an `Edit` is denied only when it touches an owned key or the `---` fence. The audit later found the hole in the key-line approach: `links.commits` and `blocked_by` list entries are not key lines, so they stayed hand-editable (A5-01, blocker). The pinned packs under `.redkiln/` were outside the deny surface as well, so editing a pack made an illegal walk legal (A9-09, A5-10). Evidence: CHANGELOG.md:2874; `src/hook.ts:160-232`; `hooks/hooks.json`; findings/_index.md A5-01, A9-09, A5-10.

- **[cli-contract] A command gate runs on both sides of its stage, and `--to` must not skip intermediate gates.** In 0.5.3 `advance --to review` jumped straight past `integration` and its failing blocking e2e gate. The engine ran the target stage's gate as an entry gate, while `project.yaml` had authored that gate as an exit gate. The two models disagreed, and the disagreement was the bypass. The fix made a command gate guard both the entry and the exit transition of its stage. Nine months later, `retier` was still landing an item "past every intermediate gate having evaluated none" (A9-07). Evidence: `references/2026-07-21-advance-to-stage-bypasses-blocking-gates.md`; `processes/story.yaml` (implement-stage comment); findings A9-07, A2-09, A2-10.

- **[human-decisions] A verdict call must bound its walk, or it overshoots past the human gate and cannot be undone.** A bare `advance <id> --verdict approved --commit` walked the item through `report` into `closeout`/`done`, because `closeout` has no gate. `advance` is forward-only, so no verb moved it back, and `validate` and `doctor` both reported the tree clean. This happened on at least 17 stories and 2 projects between 2026-08-02 and 08-06, each repaired by hand with `git revert`. The guard was a paragraph of prose in `commands/implement.md` telling the model to always pass `--stay` / `--to report`. It is the RFC's lead example of state corruption that becomes impossible once the transition is code. Evidence: reverts `59268262`, `7a8eee06`, `3461fdb9`, `f8fc12be` … `a14813fe`, `d703f33c`; `78c29dba` (diff of `.bklg/docs-site-brand-identity/_implementation.md`, "Process note"); RFC §3.3 quoting `implement.md:380-386`.

- **[testing] Every declared field of a workflow's return is checked against what its consumers read, with `always` and `sometimes` partitioned.** A command gated on `designReview`, which only the success return carried, so it read `undefined` on exactly the path where the design review had failed. `check-parse.mjs` now joins `meta.returns` five ways using an espree walk, `scan-returns.mjs`, which reaches HALT branches that a green run never takes. It fails on any return it cannot reduce to a set of keys. In Rust this becomes an `enum Outcome { Halted, Completed }` with shared always-fields. Evidence: AGENTS.md "The seam contract"; `workflows/forge-implement.js:2630`; `test/check-parse-returns-guard.test.ts`.

- **[testing] A checker must report its coverage as a ratio with a floor.** It was house policy that a checker whose green means both "found nothing wrong" and "looked at nothing" is not a checker. So the scanners publish ratios and anti-vacuity floors (`MIN_DECLARED_FIELDS = 152`, `MIN_TOPLEVEL_RETURNS`). The audit then found the floors were unpinned constants that nothing asserted (B6-04), that one coverage floor was a constant rather than a denominator (B4-10), and that a no-op refactor could silently drop the nested arm to 3 of 33 reads (B6-02). The lesson for the guard itself: pin the denominator, not just the floor. Evidence: AGENTS.md "Editing workflows"; findings B6-02, B6-04, B4-10.

- **[skills-prompts] The model tier and the reasoning effort are pinned at every dispatch layer, and an omitted effort is not a default.** An omitted `effort` is read as inherit-from-session, so before this rule "the cost and quality profile of a run was a property of the CALLER". There are three layers. Workflow `agent()` opts carry it as `TIER_POLICY`, keyed by label namespace. Agent cards carry it in frontmatter, because `Task()` takes no `effort`. Command frontmatter carries it as `COMMAND_POLICY`. The command layer was missing last, although a command is the longest-lived context in a run. `AC-ROUTE-AGENTS` and `AC-ROUTE-COMMANDS` check these in both directions. Built-in `Explore` launches remain unpinnable and are listed as named exceptions. Evidence: AGENTS.md:90, :119, :147-170; `workflows/_lib/tiers.mjs`; `agents/redkiln-review.md` frontmatter (`model: inherit` recorded as a decision).

- **[llm-judgement] Label honesty is the gap no static check closes, and a judgment step labelled `gate:` ran at haiku twice.** Routing keys on label prefixes (`gate:` haiku, `verify:` sonnet, `review:` opus). Twice a step was mislabelled. `seal:` sat at haiku while it wrote a commit that other runs depend on. The implement `gate:` relay was asked to classify failure scope from the specs, a verdict that authorised writes outside the slice. `relayViolations` now rejects a `gate:` prompt that interpolates an artifact path or lacks "do NOT edit". It catches only that shape. The rule of thumb: haiku only when relaying is the whole job and the step leaves behind nothing another step must trust. Evidence: AGENTS.md:235-245; `workflows/_lib/scan-dispatch.mjs`; RFC R13.

- **[git] The committed bundle is `-merge`, so two rebuilt bundles conflict instead of producing a bundle nobody built.** `dist/cli.js` was committed (the repo was the plugin) and was a 28,000-line generated file that was not minified. Two branches that changed different modules merged line by line, exit 0, into a file containing both changes that esbuild never emitted. `dist/** -merge` forces a conflict only when both sides changed it, and the friction was measured to be proportional. A rebuilding merge driver was rejected: it must be registered in each clone's `.git/config`, so on a fresh clone it fails open silently. `binary` was rejected because `-text` would drop the eol=lf normalisation. Evidence: `.gitattributes` (#210 block); `test/bundle-merge-attr.test.ts`.

- **[cross-platform] eol=lf is pinned because CRLF shipped in a packaged artifact and broke the Workflow harness.** On a Windows checkout with `core.autocrlf=true`, the working tree, which is what gets packaged, held CRLF `workflows/*.js`. The Workflow harness rejects a named workflow script containing carriage returns ("control characters that would be hidden in the approval dialog"). The only CRLF exception is `bin/redkiln.cmd`. Fonts are pinned `binary` explicitly rather than trusted to auto-detection. Evidence: `.gitattributes` header and font block.

- **[distribution] A release goes nowhere until the version key moves, so version sync is a test and the tag is gated on CI.** The plugin cache is keyed on the version directory. In 0.20.0 a marketplace clone sat at the fixed commit with `autoUpdate: true` while the cached `dist/cli.js` still lacked the fix, because `0.20.0/` already existed. Separately, v0.15.0 was tagged by hand while CI was red on `version-sync`, and shipped a version installers already had. `test/version-sync.test.ts` pins `plugin.json` to `package.json`. Evidence: `test/version-sync.test.ts:26-31`; readiness-ruling.md C3; CHANGELOG.md:2020-2029.

- **[process] Packs and templates are copied into the repo and pinned at init, so every pack change is a user migration.** "Upgrading the plugin must not change the rules an in-flight item is being held to." So each release carries a "Pack changes / migration" section, and `redkiln adopt --packs --templates` takes the change. The audit found the pin was hollow: "the pack pins a delegation, not a rule". `verify.ts` logic changed underneath pinned packs (A9-01, blocker). `adopt`'s in-flight guard was branch-local, one `cd` away from bypass (A9-02). And `adopt --templates` clobbered customisations (A9-04). Evidence: CHANGELOG.md:7-21; findings A9-01, A9-02, A9-04; readiness review M8.

- **[hooks] A plugin hook must not write into a repo that never opted in.** Early versions scaffolded `.redkiln/telemetry/` and `git add`ed it in any git repo opened with the plugin installed. Hooks now check for `.redkiln/` first through a 2.5 KB gate, which also keeps the 400 KB sink off the cold path of every file edit. Evidence: CHANGELOG.md:3116-3121.

## Patterns

- **[headless-agents] `reconcile` replaces `.filter(Boolean)` so a fan-out reports "N of M produced; K degraded".** `safeAgent`, `reconcile`, `degraded`/`degradedSummary` and `chunk` absorb the harness's imperfections: a null agent result is named, not silently dropped from a shorter array. The RFC singles these out to "port faithfully, do not improve" and calls `reconcile` "the single most reusable thing in the repo". Evidence: `workflows/_lib/forge-lib.js:52, :86-90`; RFC §3.2.

- **[git] Resume state lives in git trailers on two axes, because the rejected state looks identical to the passed one.** Committed stories carry `Story: <proj>/<slug>`. Sealed slices carry `Slice-Verdict: <proj>/<slice> <verdict>`. The second axis exists because "a slice REJECTED at review looks identical, in commit history, to one that passed … the state that most needs recovery was precisely the state resume could not see". Before it, a `changes-requested` slice with committed stories could not be reached on relaunch, and repair took three launches and five human interventions. The design gap left in Node: both axes were read through an LLM's summary of git (B2-08), and the review fix pass carried no trailer at all (readiness ruling C6). Evidence: `references/2026-07-22-slice-rereview-gap-and-gate-flake-budget.md`; findings B2-08; readiness-ruling.md C6; RFC §3.4.

- **[events-store] Locks and id reservations live under `<git-common-dir>/redkiln/`, so every worktree resolves the same lock.** Ids collided across worktrees until the lock and reservation ledger moved there. The RFC says this area "has been wrong three times". Known residual defects to avoid in a port: a wall-clock step treated as proof that a lock holder is dead (A5-05), cross-host stranded locks that the product cannot clear (A5-06), and a fsync of the file but not the parent directory (A5-12). Evidence: CHANGELOG.md (0.12.0 "Ids cannot collide across worktrees"); RFC §3.5 table; findings A5-05, A5-06, A5-12.

- **[events-store] There is one writer path: validate, stamp, canonical key order, lock, read the body inside the lock, match EOL, serialize, fsync a temp file, rename.** It replaced nine bare `writeFileSync` sites. Reading the body inside the lock was the actual fix ("the lock alone never was"). `updated` went to millisecond resolution after a same-second tie went red in 2 of 9 runs. Evidence: CHANGELOG.md:2880-2885 (0.12.0 "One writer"); RFC §3.5 (`store/write.ts`).

- **[events-store] Telemetry has one partition per writer and is committed with `merge=union`, scoped to exactly the generated paths.** Partitions are named `<actor>@<worktree>.jsonl`. Union is needed only when one partition name exists on two branches; this was measured, and distinct names merge cleanly with no attribute. The hand-authored fixture is explicitly held out of union, and `git check-attr` asserts both halves. The hazard: the index deduplicates by name, keeping the longest copy, so one-writer-per-name is the load-bearing invariant. Issue #64 broke it by redirecting an append across trees: 67 runs on disk, 17 reported. Evidence: `.gitattributes` (telemetry block); CHANGELOG.md:2031-2045; `test/telemetry-committed.test.ts`.

- **[testing] The memo key folds in exactly the inputs each check reads, enumerated beside the checks.** The gate memo was keyed on tree identity, which excludes `.bklg`. Editing `_ledger.md` to `satisfied: false` made `verify` exit 1 while `advance` reused the memo and advanced at rc 0 with `basis: memoized`. The fix enumerates named inputs (`spec.md`, `_ledger.md`, `links.commits`) via `verifyIdentity`. A directory-wide digest was rejected because `advance` renders artifacts into that directory between preflight and confirm. A flaked run must not be memoized. Evidence: CHANGELOG.md:1671-1690; RFC §3.5 (`verify-cache.ts`); finding A4-04 (`verifyIdentity` later dead code).

- **[observability] `gate_result.basis` distinguishes evaluated, memoized and warned.** Without it, a non-blocking failure was indistinguishable from a real pass. It was dropped again on the unlocked preflight path (A2-03), and `not-ready` got laundered into a verdict about the change (A3-02). Keep "measured and failed", "could not evaluate" and "skipped" as distinct values all the way to telemetry. Evidence: CHANGELOG.md (0.12.0 Added, `basis`); findings A2-03, A3-02, A3-06.

- **[skills-prompts] The context rule is literal versus associative recall, not a fill percentage.** Read distilled artifacts (context packs, digests) inline. For associative recall across an undistilled corpus, dispatch a fresh read-only subagent that writes long output to disk and returns a digest. "Convert associative recall into literal recall wherever you can." Context packs state the decision rather than naming the file that holds it. Evidence: `skills/conventions/SKILL.md:44-66`; AGENTS.md:147-160 (Explore launches justified by it).

- **[skills-prompts] A skill is scoped with `paths:`, never with a claim that it auto-loads.** The conventions skill once claimed it was "auto-loaded so Claude follows Redkiln rules"; nothing loaded it, and the rules were enforced only where a command restated them. It now uses `paths: [.bklg/**, .kb/**, .redkiln/**]` and states that the hook is the enforcement and the skill is the explanation. Evidence: `skills/conventions/SKILL.md:1-23`.

- **[llm-judgement] The adversarial reviewer card names an escape-hatch taxonomy and denies that merged precedent blesses a hatch.** `redkiln-review` defaults to skepticism and must trace each AC to reachable behaviour ("constructed is not integrated"). It treats doubles standing in for a needed contract, fixture-pinned answers, `test.fixme` or flag-gated scenarios, and unmounted deliverables as blocking. Its step 5 carve-out allows in-repo precedent for real conventions but never for a gaming pattern merged earlier ("that is the precedent propagating the defect"). Field reports call this reviewer "the single best part of the run": it caught a regression the operator had reported green. Evidence: `agents/redkiln-review.md:26-90`; `references/2026-07-22-slice-rereview-gap-and-gate-flake-budget.md` summary.

- **[process] A field report is harvested into a dated file with a disposition table, re-verified at HEAD.** Six reports were written to a consumer's `research/` folder and none became a backlog item; one was refiled verbatim the next day. The fix built an intake half (issue template, how-to) and a harvest half: a filed report plus a table mapping each finding to the change that closed it. Rule: an observation from an older version may motivate work but may never prove a gap. Evidence: `references/field-reports/2026-07-25-closeout-lane-findings.md:9-48`.

- **[cost-capacity] Spend ranks volume first, then cache, then tier.** Output price runs 1:3:5 across the tiers, but measured output-token volume varied 180x across 67 runs (15,417 to 2,774,995), so volume beats tier by about 36x. A cold versus warm cache swings about 12.5x. Tier and effort are pinned anyway because they are free to check, not because they are where the money is. The audit added a caveat: tier was never attributed to spend, so "36x" compares two different axes (B4-08). Evidence: AGENTS.md:73-88; `workflows/_lib/tiers.mjs:52-60`; finding B4-08.

## Gotchas

- **[headless-agents] The Workflow runtime's resume journal keys on (prompt, opts), so a re-dispatch replays the earlier verdict.** After a fix pass, re-running review with an identical prompt cache-hit and "replays the PRE-fix verdict — passing the gate on code the fix already changed". Every non-idempotent re-dispatch had to mutate its label (`#retry${n}`, `:${attempt}`). Evidence: `workflows/forge-implement.js:2068-2071`; RFC §3.2.

- **[headless-agents] A transport failure was recorded as a rejection, written into git.** "One HTTP 529 produced a COMMITTED `changes-requested` seal whose entire surviving finding was 'review failed to produce a digest'." `!review` meant both "the reviewer said no" and "the reviewer never spoke". This is the RK-B0013 class; it becomes `Result<Verdict, TransportError>`. The same class survived elsewhere: a `not-ready` story gate (exit 2) was sealed as `changes-requested` (B2-03). Evidence: `workflows/forge-implement.js:2150, :2175-2181`; finding B2-03.

- **[cli-contract] Workflow `args` arrive as an object or as a JSON string depending on the launch path.** When the string went unparsed, `coerceArgs` produced `{}` and `slug` became `undefined`. The workflow fanned out 9 subagents writing toward `.bklg/undefined/…`, spending about 361k tokens and 166 s, and reported `completed:unverified` instead of aborting. Fix: validate the seed shape before any fan-out (measured: 9 dispatches without the guard, 0 with it). Evidence: `references/2026-07-20-initiative-workflow-fail-fast.md`; `workflows/_lib/forge-lib.js:307-314`; field report finding #2 (`34af4008`).

- **[cost-capacity] The Workflow runtime gives one cumulative `budget.spent()` and no per-agent usage.** Per-agent cost had to be rebuilt by hand (`agentCensus`, `modelMix`, about 120 LOC). Those counters were then wrong: `agentsRetried` and `agentsFailed` read keys the census never emits, so they showed 0 in all 153 real runs, against a test asserting an invented fixture (A7-01). Budgets were recorded on every run and set on none (B4-12). Evidence: `workflows/_lib/forge-lib.js:34-37`; findings A7-01, B4-12.

- **[cross-platform] On Windows, `mkdir` against a just-deleted directory throws `EPERM`, not `EEXIST`.** An atomic-mkdir lock failed about 0.3% of contended acquisitions (11 of 3,600) because Windows leaves an `rmdir`ed directory pending deletion. Release already retried `EPERM`/`EBUSY` and acquire did not; the asymmetry was closed in #212. `EACCES`, `ENOENT` and `ENOSPC` still fail fast. Evidence: CHANGELOG.md:160-180.

- **[github-ci] On private-repo GitHub runners (2 vCPU) vitest runs serially, unnoticed.** The default `maxWorkers` resolved to 1, so every leg ran serially. Per-file durations summed to wall-clock time, and the Windows leg took 89 minutes, 87 of them in `vitest run`. Nothing reported it because serial and parallel runs print the same summary. Process spawn on Windows cost about 210 ms per fixture repo (#160). Evidence: CHANGELOG.md:780-823, :715-733.

- **[cross-platform] A command gate with Node's default `maxBuffer` (1 MiB) reports a successful noisy build as failed.** `execFileSync(..., {shell: true})` with no `maxBuffer` and no timeout: 2 MiB of output with exit 0 failed the gate, and a hung gate blocked `advance` forever. The opposite failure, false green, was trusting the exit code from toolchains where it is not a verdict (UnrealEditor-Cmd). Even after the fix, the timeout killed the shell, not the runner beneath it (A4-09). Evidence: `references/2026-07-24-native-repo-support-seed.md` (Defects 1-2); finding A4-09.

- **[trust-security] The evidence-citation guard had a web-only extension whitelist and rejected 57% of Rust citations.** The `verify.ts` regex accepted `ts|js|md|yaml|json|…` and no `.rs`, `.toml`, `.py` or `.go`. Across happenstance's live branches, 566 of 990 satisfied rows citing `.rs` failed it. `require_ledger: false` did not turn it off. This is a default chosen from the dogfood repo's language, then shipped to everyone. Evidence: readiness-ruling.md C1 (`src/store/verify.ts:1599-1601`, `:1831-1844`); finding A4-01.

- **[skills-prompts] A template section the gate parses was never shipped, so greenfield repos had two of four checks inert, silently.** `templates/spec.md` never carried `## PR boundary` (`git log -S` over all history is empty). Only happenstance had hand-authored it. An unparseable boundary degraded to an advisory and switched provenance off, with no error and no doctor finding (A4-07). The audit filed this, its most consequential adoption defect, only in a verifier's appendix. Evidence: readiness-ruling.md C2; findings A4-07, A4-06, B3-04.

- **[cross-platform] The `node:sqlite` builtin is experimental and was loaded eagerly.** It emits `ExperimentalWarning`, and when it is unavailable every command breaks, including `--version` (A6-04). The RFC counts `rusqlite` bundled as a straight win. Evidence: readiness review "Other"; finding A6-04; RFC §3.5.

- **[git] Git spawns dominate cost: a measured 160-second lock hold, mostly spent in git.** "84% of the cost of a lock measured holding for 160 SECONDS against a 5,000 ms acquisition budget" (`src/store/ids.ts:196`). Batching blob reads with `cat-file --batch` cut about 1,100 spawns to about 22. Evidence: RFC §3.5 (`git/`); CHANGELOG.md (0.12.0 "`--all-branches` reads blobs in one batched `cat-file` pass").

- **[hooks] A Node hook cold-starts the whole bundle on every Write/Edit.** The "whole gate architecture" (a 2.5 KB pre-gate in front of the 400–474 KB sink) existed because every edit repo-wide ran `node dist/hook.js`. A compiled binary removes the reason. Evidence: RFC §3.5 (`hook.ts` + `emit.ts`); CHANGELOG.md:3119-3121; `hooks/hooks.json`.

- **[observability] The worktree label in telemetry was the CWD basename, not the worktree.** A run in happenstance's `crates/` directory minted worktree `"crates"`. Resolve the worktree root; never use `basename(cwd)`. Evidence: finding A7-03 (`src/store/actor.ts:103`).

- **[plugin-packaging] A plugin cannot declare that it requires another MCP server.** Commands were granted tools from a third-party `context-mode` MCP plugin that the repo neither shipped nor could declare. An install without it got commands whose granted tools did not exist. They were replaced with the built-in `Explore`. Evidence: AGENTS.md:158-165.

- **[trust-security] The reviewer's "no write access" was nominal.** `tools: [Read, Bash]` with Bash unscoped, a hook that cannot see Bash writes, and a workflow that required the reviewer to write (B5-02). `Bash(git commit:*)` permits `--no-verify`, which five commands forbid in prose only (B1-14). `Bash(redkiln:*)` pre-approved every state mutation (B1-08). Evidence: findings B5-02, B1-14, B1-08; `agents/redkiln-review.md:13`.

## Lessons from history

- **[headless-agents] A subagent tried an unauthorised self-repair of a corrupted shared `.git/config`.** Mid-run, `.git/config` (shared by the primary checkout and 10 worktrees) was found overwritten with 519 NUL bytes, likely a disk fault. Git broke repo-wide for every concurrent session. The integration agent attempted a guessed raw rewrite, which the harness flagged. The proper fix verified the remote through `gh api` and `FETCH_HEAD` and got explicit human sign-off before writing. Lesson: agents must halt, not repair, on shared git infrastructure. Evidence: `78c29dba` (commit message and RK-P0021 run 4 note).

- **[llm-judgement] Repair passes rewrote the acceptance criterion, or the review record, to fit what shipped.** A slice repair pass reworded an acceptance sentence to match the implementation, and both review layers passed it (C2-03). A fix story's recorded provenance was the commit that narrowed its own ACs (C2-01). The producer's fix pass was instructed to rewrite the durable review record (B5-04). A fixer must never write to the spec or verdict it is judged by. assay rediscovered this as trust paths over its corpus (assay decisions log, 2026-10-14). Evidence: findings C2-03, C2-01, B5-04.

- **[llm-judgement] The reviewer's verdict is unstable while its findings are stable, so the verdict string must not be the blocking bit.** Every gaming detector the reviewer filled in was advisory, and only the verdict string carried weight (B5-01). There was no judge-bias control or negative-control fixture; the harness only ever exercised a compliant reviewer (B5-06). The 23/23 approved in the fix lane was a selection effect, because the lane could not persist `changes-requested` (B5-05). These fed redkiln-rs F27 (verdict flips on 2 of 4 items, 0 of 7 detection flips) and ADR-0001's move to policy over typed findings. Evidence: findings B5-01, B5-05, B5-06; redkiln-rs `references/adr/0001-inversion-architecture-bet.md`.

- **[observability] The audit's own scorer silently scored 5 of 33 dimensions.** The 46-agent audit published 66.7/100. The collator scraped criteria from markdown, where they were never written, instead of from structured digests. A verifier exhausted its structured-output retry cap and its verdicts were dropped. The corrected score was 51.8. "This audit shipped the same class of defect it charges redkiln with": a control that ran, produced output, and could not fail visibly. A human critic caught it by reading the arithmetic cold. Evidence: `references/audit/2026-08-30-state-of-the-system/scoresheet.md` §7; README.md "Scoring corrected".

- **[process] An artifact with no reader gets filled with unauthored template.** Of 430 `plan.md` files across three repos, 370 were the byte-identical template. A proposal to add a gate to `plan` was reversed: it "would have authored ~430 essays with zero consumers". `plan` now `produces: []`. The same audit found `story.md` cards left as stubs while `spec.md` held the substance, which read as a failed run. Companion `status:` frontmatter was deleted from 18 templates because nothing updated it and it looked authoritative. Evidence: `processes/story.yaml` (plan-stage comment); `references/2026-07-20-story-md-card-left-as-stub.md`; field report finding #6.

- **[distribution] Readiness blocker B1: the repo-as-plugin was broken from a clean clone.** `dist/` was gitignored while `hooks.json`, `bin` and `main` all pointed into it, and `zod`/`yaml` were externalised. Hooks failed silently at every event. The fix committed `dist/`, bundled the dependencies and gated CI on `git diff --exit-code -- dist/`. The cost came later: stale `dist/` drift between projects (255 lines in RK-P0021) and the `-merge` attribute. The RFC treats "clone and it works" as the one property a compiled binary permanently loses. The suite's answer is binaries in `libexec/` behind a launcher. Evidence: `REDKILN-V1-READINESS-REVIEW.md` B1, M5; `78c29dba` (MD-2); RFC §3.5 "The permanent loss".

- **[cli-contract] Readiness blocker B2: three read commands had three freshness contracts.** `status` rebuilt the index only when the database was absent, while `board` and `view` rebuilt every run, despite identical "always reflects current state" docstrings. A malformed item made `reindex` exit 1 while `status` exited 0 with a clean board. The remedy: one `openFreshIndex()` helper. Unknown flags were silently ignored (`parseArgs strict:false`), so `--commmit` became a dry run. And `doctor --fix-ids --dry-run` parsed `dryRun` but never forwarded it, so ids were rewritten. Evidence: readiness review B2, M2, M7; RFC §3.5 (`cli/` row).

- **[trust-security] Committed telemetry: M6 was reversed, and the privacy reasoning was kept.** The review said to remove committed personal telemetry. 0.15.0 (#65) reversed this: a run ledger is only useful if it is shared, and while gitignored, `record-run` warned on every call that the run was local-only. The written reasoning: only the email local-part reaches the path; commit metadata already publishes it; the event bodies (an activity trace including uncommitted work) are the new exposure; so the opt-out must stay real, as an advisory and never a failure. Evidence: `REDKILN-V1-READINESS-REVIEW.md` M6 note (2026-08-07); `src/store/run-log.ts`; `test/telemetry-committed.test.ts`.

- **[testing] Whole-initiative definition-of-done gates halted every non-terminal project.** In a "foundation projects plus one assembly project" decomposition, all the DoD journeys belong to the terminal project. Every feature project failed `dodGreen` by construction, and the verdict required it. The fix made the DoD terminal-aware, with a project-scoped bar for feature projects. A related lesson: an e2e gate sensitive to parallel load produced four different failing pairs in five runs, while `--workers=1` was clean every time, and there was no flake budget. Evidence: `references/2026-07-20-implement-integration-feedback.md` Item 1; `references/2026-07-22-slice-rereview-gap-and-gate-flake-budget.md` Issue 2; finding C3-01.

- **[process] Prose-only orchestration made recovery impossible after partial completion, repeatedly.** `/redkiln:plan` threw on any re-run after its first mutation and committed nothing (B1-03). Closeout's five gated mutations had no recovery from partial completion (B1-04), and its landing call could not execute because nothing advanced the initiative to `review` (B1-01). `/redkiln:initiative` had no resume path (B1-12). Each stage of a lane must be re-entrant from durable state. Evidence: findings B1-01, B1-03, B1-04, B1-12.

- **[human-decisions] The control loop worked when it halted correctly, which is not the same as a stuck pipeline.** In happenstance, HS-P0013 committed 12 of 12 stories, and its halt on two irreducibly human actions was correct. docs-that-teach halted because the operator refused to reshape a fence to clear a gate and filed the defect upstream (PR #153). HS-S0158 was blocked by a signed-off design budget (130 `//!` lines) that a sibling project had used up, a constraint nothing modelled (C1-01). Evidence: readiness-ruling.md §1, §3; finding C1-01.

- **[process] A fix release can break the resume it was meant to enable.** Moving the plugin from `v0.20.0` to the fixing commit introduced the citation guard, which flipped two of the "high confidence" passing stories to fail. The new binary with old pinned packs was "exercised by no test, appears in no recorded run". Require a mixed-version test before any relaunch. Evidence: readiness-ruling.md §1, C5.

## Open problems

- **[llm-judgement] Mapping work to labels.** No checker verifies that a step's label honestly describes its work. `relayViolations` catches one shape only, and the RFC admits that a type system cannot tell whether a prompt asks a judgment question (R13). Evidence: AGENTS.md:233-245; RFC R13.

- **[cost-capacity] Reasoning effort below `high` was never measured.** `low`/`medium` stayed unused because "a downward tune is a quality bet this repo cannot yet measure" without a p90. The audit noted that the stated precondition had been met and nothing was done (B4-11). Prompt-cache hit rate was never instrumented. Evidence: AGENTS.md:101-106; finding B4-11.

- **[testing] Per-story fences inside a slice that shares a module.** The ledger records it as "worth fixing at plan grain rather than amending per project a fourth time", and nothing at 0.20.0 addressed it. Evidence: readiness-ruling.md §3 (HS-P0023 "Also still owed").

- **[events-store] The hash chain covered 9% of redkiln's own ledger and 0% of happenstance's.** Every property resting on it was aspirational (A7-11). The evidence ledger was an unsynchronized read-modify-write of the chain head, with no repair for a fork (A5-03). Evidence: findings A7-11, A5-03, A5-04.

- **[human-decisions] `approver: human` was opt-in to auto-approval.** The one surface with real evidence of human presence (the decision endpoint) discarded it (A3-03). The AI reviewer held a veto over whether the human project gate ever fired (B1-15). Evidence: findings A3-03, B1-15.

- **[distribution] Harness output contracts are undocumented and mutable.** Documented `-p -r` hangs, silent server-side output changes, and every surveyed multi-harness tool ending up with permissions bypassed. MULTI-HARNESS recommends a version matrix plus golden transcripts, or a contract of exit codes and files on disk only, and moving safety to the boundary (worktrees, diff review, the gate). It also warns that the moat must be "the state machine, the KB substrate, and the audit trail — not the orchestration loop", since harnesses absorb orchestration (agent-os shut down for that reason). Evidence: `references/MULTI-HARNESS.md` §5.1-5.4.
